Read this before trusting the numbers: every asset class here is driven entirely by the growth rate you typed in — none of it is a prediction. Crypto and equities can swing wildly from year to year even when their long-run average matches your input; property leverage cuts both ways and can amplify losses if values fall. This model also ignores income tax, capital gains tax, and (for property) maintenance surprises or refinancing. Treat it as a way to compare assumptions consistently, not as investment advice.

How it's calculated

Residential & industrial property (leveraged)

Your capital is the down payment, so the property controlled is worth Capital / Down Payment %, financed with an amortizing loan for the rest. Each year's net income is Property Value × Net Yield %; subtract that year's mortgage payments to get cash flow. At the end of the horizon, the property is sold: Sale Price − Selling Costs − Remaining Loan Balance is added to the cash flow collected along the way.

For an industrial property bought BUC, net income is zero for every year before "rental starts in year" — the property still appreciates and the loan still amortizes during that stretch, but there's no rent to collect until construction completes.

Financial vehicles & crypto (unleveraged)

Price return, yield, and ongoing fees combine into one net annual growth rate, assumed to compound (yield reinvested rather than taken as cash — the common default for index funds and staking). Exit costs are deducted from the final value only.

Total ROI & annualized return

Total ROI = Total Profit / Capital Invested × 100 for all four. For leveraged property, annualized return is the true IRR of the full cash flow stream (capital out, yearly cash flow, sale proceeds) — it accounts for money coming back to you along the way. For financial vehicles and crypto, with no interim cash flow, annualized return is simply the compound annual growth rate (CAGR) of the investment.
