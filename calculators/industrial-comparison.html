Industrial Property: New vs Resale

Compare a brand new industrial unit against a resale one in Singapore — GST, stamp duty, financing, and total cash required — with every category placed side by side so the differences are obvious.

Two rules unique to industrial/commercial property: CPF cannot be used for the purchase — it's cash and bank loan only — and ABSD doesn't apply (that's a residential-only duty). Buyer's Stamp Duty still applies, at the non-residential rates (capped at 4%, lower than residential's top tiers).

How it's calculated

Buyer's Stamp Duty (non-residential)

Industrial and other non-residential property uses a flatter schedule than residential: 1% on the first S$180,000, 2% on the next S$180,000, 3% on the next S$640,000, and 4% on the remainder — with no further escalation, unlike residential property's higher tiers above S$1.5m/S$3m. ABSD does not apply to industrial property at all.

GST

GST is charged when the seller is GST-registered — almost always true for a developer selling new units, and only sometimes true on the resale market (e.g. a company-owned unit). It's computed on the purchase price and is a cash cost — it can't be financed into the mortgage.

Financing

CPF cannot be used for industrial or other non-residential property — the downpayment and every upfront cost (GST, stamp duty, legal, agent, renovation) must be paid in cash. Loan tenure is also capped at the property's remaining lease, since a bank won't lend past the point the lease runs out.

Total cash required

Total Cash Required = Downpayment + Total Upfront Costs — the headline number for "how much money do I actually need to complete this purchase," which is what the comparison table's differences are built around.
