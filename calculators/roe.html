Why ROE matters: as a property appreciates and the loan pays down, your equity grows — but cash flow usually doesn't keep pace. The same dollars of profit sitting on a larger pile of equity means a shrinking return. A falling ROE trend is often the signal that it's time to refinance, sell, or 1031 exchange into a new property so that equity keeps working as hard as it did on day one.

How it's calculated

Equity

Equity = Property Value − Loan Balance

Cash-flow ROE

Cash-Flow ROE = Annual Cash Flow / Beginning-of-Year Equity × 100

Total ROE

Total ROE = (Annual Cash Flow + Appreciation Gain) / Beginning-of-Year Equity × 100, where appreciation gain is the property's value increase during that year. This captures your full economic return — cash in hand plus equity growth — relative to the equity you have invested.

Projection

Property value compounds at your appreciation rate. The loan balance is amortized forward from its current balance using the interest rate and years remaining — the same fixed monthly payment that would pay it off on schedule. Cash flow compounds at your cash flow growth rate. Each year's ROE divides that year's return by the equity you had at the start of the year, matching how return on capital is conventionally measured.
