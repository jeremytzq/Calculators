Read this before trusting the numbers: capital appreciation is your assumption, not a forecast — Singapore residential prices have had multi-year stretches both well above and well below 4% p.a. This model ignores income tax on rental income, seller's stamp duty (SSD, if you sell within the holding period that triggers it), CPF usage, and the fact that agent commission here is a flat % of the original purchase price rather than the eventual sale price. Treat "equity available" as a planning estimate, not a loan pre-approval.

How it's calculated

Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD)

Singapore's progressive residential BSD: 1% on the first S$180,000, 2% on the next S$180,000, 3% on the next S$640,000, 4% on the next S$500,000, 5% on the next S$1,500,000, and 6% on anything above S$3,000,000.

Monthly instalment & outstanding mortgage

Standard amortizing loan on Purchase Price × (1 − Down Payment %). The outstanding balance at exit is the remaining principal after your holding period's worth of payments.

Equity available as next down payment

Sale Price + Net Cashflow Over Holding − Outstanding Mortgage − Stamp Duty − ABSD − Conveyancing − Agent Commission − Vacancy Loss. This is the cash you'd walk away with after selling and settling every cost tracked above — the pool you could redeploy as a down payment on your next purchase.

Next property value & leverage

Next Property Value = Equity Available / Next Property Down Payment %. Leverage expresses that as a percentage of your original purchase price, showing how much bigger a property the extracted equity could control.
