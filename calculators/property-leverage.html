Read this before trusting the numbers: capital appreciation is your assumption, not a forecast — Singapore residential prices have had multi-year stretches both well above and well below 4% p.a. Rent and holding costs are held flat in nominal terms (no growth rate). This model ignores income tax on rental income, Seller's Stamp Duty (SSD, which can apply if you sell within a few years of purchase), and CPF usage. Treat "equity available" and "break-even" as planning estimates, not a loan pre-approval. For BUC in particular, "equity" can look unusually high in the first year or two — property value already assumes full appreciation from day one, while the loan is still mostly undisbursed and mortgage costs are mostly cheap interest-only-scale amounts. That gap is a paper artifact of the model's assumptions, not cash you could actually bank early.

How it's calculated

Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD)

Singapore's progressive residential BSD: 1% on the first S$180,000, 2% on the next S$180,000, 3% on the next S$640,000, 4% on the next S$500,000, 5% on the next S$1,500,000, and 6% on anything above S$3,000,000.

BUC vs. resale

Resale properties earn rent from year 1, with the loan fully disbursed at purchase. A BUC (Building Under Construction) purchase earns no rent until the "rental starts in year" you set (typically the year construction reaches TOP) — every year before that has holding costs with zero rental income to offset them. The loan itself also disburses progressively, not all at once — see below.

Progressive payment schedule (BUC)

Singapore BUC purchases follow a standard stage-by-stage disbursement set by the Housing Developers Rules: your down payment covers everything up to booking/S&P (typically 20%), and the bank loan then disburses in stages as construction hits milestones — Foundation, framework, walls, roofing, TOP, and finally CSC. We scale the standard stage timing so TOP lands on your "rental starts in year," and each stage disburses its stated % of the loan (not the purchase price).

Monthly instalment, principal & interest

Standard amortizing loan on Purchase Price × (1 − Down Payment %). For a resale purchase, the loan is fully disbursed from month 1 and this is a plain amortization schedule: each payment splits into principal (which becomes equity immediately) and interest (a pure cost), shifting toward principal as the loan matures.

For BUC, each month before full disbursement, interest is charged only on what's actually been disbursed so far, and the instalment is what a fresh loan of that disbursed amount would cost over your full loan term — the portion above interest is treated as a real paydown of what's owed, so less is actually left once the loan is fully disbursed at CSC than the nominal loan amount. From full disbursement onward, it's standard amortization on whatever balance remains, over the same original loan term.

Vacancy & net cash flow

Vacancy reduces rental income in every year rent is being collected — Net Rental = Gross Rental × (1 − Vacancy %) — before it ever reaches the cash flow line, rather than being deducted once as a lump sum at the end.

Equity & break-even

Equity = Property Value − Loan Balance each year. "Net position vs. cash invested" is what you'd walk away with if you sold that year — Equity + Cumulative Cash Flow − Selling Costs (at that year's value) − Cash Invested at Purchase — the first year this turns positive is your break-even point.

Equity available as next down payment

Sale Price − Selling Costs − Outstanding Mortgage + Cumulative Cash Flow − Stamp Duty − ABSD − Conveyancing, where selling costs (agent commission) are computed on the actual projected sale price for that scenario, not the original purchase price.

Next property value & leverage

Next Property Value = Equity Available / Next Property Down Payment %. Leverage expresses that as a percentage of your original purchase price, showing how much bigger a property the extracted equity could control.
